# Organizational Network Analysis Questionnaire

This questionnaire has two parts.

**Part A** is the participant-facing survey instrument: the roster questions you
actually send to people to collect network data.

**Part B** is a readiness and ethics check for the practitioner, scored 1 (not
ready) to 5 (ready). **Complete Part B before sending Part A.** ONA data is the
most sensitive material in this collection, and a badly run ONA does lasting
damage.

See [organizational-network-analysis](../organizational-network-analysis/).

**Evidence: Good as a method.** Social network analysis is a mature, validated
research methodology with robust organizational findings. The weaknesses are
practical: response rates below roughly 70% make a map unreliable, and reported
ties differ from actual ties.


## Part B: Practitioner readiness check — do this first

### B1. Purpose

- Question: Can we state, in one sentence, the decision this data will inform?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

- Question: Would that decision genuinely change depending on the result?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

### B2. Consent

- Question: Will every participant be told what is collected, who sees it, how
  it is reported, and how long it is kept — before they answer?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

- Question: Is there a genuine opt-out with no consequence, and will
  non-participation be invisible to managers?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

### B3. Reporting boundaries

- Question: Have we committed in writing that no individual-level map will be
  shown to any manager about their own reports?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

- Question: Have we committed that this data will never be used in performance,
  promotion, or redundancy decisions?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

### B4. Method

- Question: Are we using a consented survey rather than passive analysis of
  email, chat, or calendar data?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

- Question: Do we expect a response rate above 70%? Below that, the map is
  unreliable and should not be presented as one.
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

### B5. Data handling

- Question: Are retention period, storage location, deletion date, and
  applicable regime (GDPR/UK GDPR, HIPAA, works council or union agreements) all
  settled?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

**Any B2 or B3 item scoring below 5 stops the exercise.** These are not
trade-offs; they are preconditions. See [od-ethics](../od-ethics/).


## Part A: The participant survey

Introduce it with the exact confidentiality terms agreed in Part B. Then ask
only the networks you will actually use — each additional network multiplies
burden and lowers response rate.

Suggested cap: three networks, five names each.

### A1. Information network

- Question: Who do you go to for the information you need to do your job? Name
  up to five people.
  1. ____________________
  2. ____________________
  3. ____________________
  4. ____________________
  5. ____________________

### A2. Advice and expertise network

- Question: Whose technical or professional judgment do you seek when you are
  stuck? Name up to five people.
  1. ____________________
  2. ____________________
  3. ____________________
  4. ____________________
  5. ____________________

### A3. Trust network

- Question: Who would you tell about a mistake you had made, before it became
  public? Name up to five people.
  1. ____________________
  2. ____________________
  3. ____________________
  4. ____________________
  5. ____________________

This is usually the smallest network, the least like the org chart, and the most
predictive of whether a change will take hold. It is also the most sensitive.
Only ask it if Part B scored clean.

### A4. Energy network (optional)

- Question: Who do you come away from an interaction with feeling more motivated
  and capable? Name up to five people.

### A5. Innovation network (optional)

- Question: Who do you talk to when you have a half-formed idea? Name up to five
  people.

### A6. Friction items (optional, scored)

- Question: I can easily find out who knows what in this organization.
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]
- Question: When I need something from another team, I know who to ask.
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]
- Question: I have the connections I need to do my job well.
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step C: Analysis

Report patterns and roles. Do not report named rankings unless the named person
has agreed.

* **Central connectors** — high in-degree. Often not senior, often overloaded,
  often invisible to leadership. Check their workload before celebrating them,
  and check whether the organization has accidentally made one person a
  bottleneck.

* **Brokers and boundary spanners** — the people connecting otherwise separate
  clusters. Removing one disconnects parts of the organization. These are the
  highest-value and most under-recognized people in most networks. Find out
  whether their brokering is in their job description; usually it is not.

* **Silos** — dense internal ties, sparse external ties. Predicts handoff
  failures precisely at the boundary. Compare against your system or service
  boundaries; the correspondence is usually uncomfortable. See
  [conways-law](../conways-law/).

* **Peripheral nodes** — weakly connected. Distinguish the three causes before
  acting: new joiners still connecting, deep specialists who are fine, and
  people quietly disengaging. Do not assume the third.

* **Bus factor** — any single node whose removal disconnects a cluster is a risk
  register item, not just a network observation.

* **Compare the trust network to the org chart.** The gap between them is the
  single most useful output of an ONA, and the one most likely to change how
  leadership plans a change programme.

* **Recruit early adopters from the map, not from the hierarchy.** Change
  spreads through respected connectors. See
  [rogers-diffusion-of-innovations](../rogers-diffusion-of-innovations/).

* **Report a response rate with every map.** Below 70%, say plainly that the map
  is indicative and not reliable, every time it is presented. Attractive network
  diagrams invite over-interpretation.

* **Delete the raw ties on the agreed date.** Then say publicly that you have.
