# Job characteristics model

Richard Hackman and Greg Oldham's job characteristics model (1975) specifies
what makes work motivating, and it is one of the best-supported models in
organizational psychology. Its practical strength: it targets the **design of
the work itself**, which is more durable than motivating the person.

**Evidence: Strong.** Meta-analytically supported across decades and many
samples: the five characteristics relate reliably to satisfaction, motivation,
and performance, and the mediating psychological states have held up
reasonably well. Two caveats are real. Most measurement is self-report, so
perception and objective job design are entangled. And growth need strength as
a moderator has produced inconsistent results, so do not assume enrichment
helps everyone equally — ask.

Use when: engagement is poor and the causes are not pay, safety, or management
behavior; when designing roles or team boundaries; when automation is changing
what a job contains.

Do not use when: the constraint is workload volume rather than work design.


## The five core characteristics

1. **Skill variety** — the degree to which the job requires different activities
   and talents.
2. **Task identity** — the degree to which the job requires completing a whole,
   identifiable piece of work with a visible outcome.
3. **Task significance** — the degree to which the job substantially affects
   other people's lives or work.
4. **Autonomy** — the degree of freedom in scheduling and determining procedures.
5. **Feedback from the job itself** — the degree to which doing the work
   provides direct information about performance. Note: from the *work*, not
   from a manager. This is the most commonly missed characteristic.


## The three critical psychological states

* Variety, identity, and significance → **experienced meaningfulness** of work.
* Autonomy → **experienced responsibility** for outcomes.
* Feedback → **knowledge of actual results**.

All three states must be present for high internal motivation. This is why
autonomy alone does not fix a job that is meaningless, and why meaningful work
without feedback leaves people unable to improve.


## Motivating potential score

```
MPS = ((Skill variety + Task identity + Task significance) / 3)
      × Autonomy
      × Feedback
```

The multiplication is the point: **autonomy or feedback near zero collapses the
score regardless of the other three.** A meaningful job with no autonomy and no
feedback is not motivating, however important it is.


## Moderator: growth need strength

The effects are stronger for people with high growth need strength — those who
want development and challenge. Enriching the job of someone who does not want
enrichment can increase stress rather than motivation. Ask.


## Implementing principles

Hackman and Oldham's five implementing concepts:

* **Combine tasks** — reverse fragmentation; restore whole pieces of work.
* **Form natural work units** — give a person or team ownership of a coherent
  domain, client group, or product.
* **Establish client relationships** — connect the worker directly to the person
  they serve. Powerful for both significance and feedback.
* **Vertical loading** — give planning and control responsibilities that were
  previously the manager's.
* **Open feedback channels** — make results visible directly from the work.


## Examples by audience

* **Health care**: task identity is damaged when care is fragmented across
  handoffs — no one clinician sees the patient's whole journey or outcome.
  Named-nurse and continuity-of-care models are direct applications, as is
  simply telling a team what happened to the patients they treated. Feedback
  from the work is frequently absent in acute settings, and its absence is
  demoralizing in a way staff struggle to articulate.

* **Software**: "you build it, you run it" raises task identity, autonomy, and
  feedback simultaneously. Conversely, a team that only writes code to someone
  else's specification, hands it to QA, and never sees production has low
  identity, low autonomy, and no feedback from the work — MPS near zero, whatever
  the salary. Fast pipelines and observability are feedback mechanisms, not just
  engineering conveniences. See [team-topologies](../team-topologies/).

* **Executive**: when automating or outsourcing, examine what the remaining job
  contains. Removing the interesting fragments and leaving exception handling
  destroys skill variety and task identity, and the resulting attrition is
  usually attributed to something else.


## Limitations

* Mostly self-report measures, so perception and job design are entangled.
* Weaker for jobs with genuinely little discretion.
* Says nothing about workload, pay, or interpersonal conditions.
* Growth need strength as a moderator has inconsistent support.
