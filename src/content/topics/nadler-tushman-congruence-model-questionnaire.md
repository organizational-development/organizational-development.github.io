# Nadler-Tushman Congruence Model Questionnaire

To run a congruence diagnostic, use this questionnaire. It assesses the four
components, then scores the six pairwise fits between them, from 1 (severe
misfit) to 5 (strong fit).

The model's premise is that performance comes from **fit**, not from component
quality. A world-class component that does not fit the others reduces
effectiveness. So the component section is preparation; the fit section is the
diagnosis.

See [nadler-tushman-congruence-model](../nadler-tushman-congruence-model/).

**Evidence: Moderate.** The specific instrument is not validated, but the
underlying contingency-theory principle has real support. Use the fit scores to
locate an argument, not to settle one.


## Step 1: Establish the outputs first

Before diagnosing, name what is actually underperforming. Diagnosing fit without
a defined output produces an interesting document and no decision.

1. System level

- Question: What organizational result is below where it needs to be, and by how
  much?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Unit level

- Question: Which specific units are underperforming, and which are not?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Individual level

- Question: What is happening to individual performance, satisfaction, and
  turnover?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 2: Describe the four components

1. Work

- Question: What is the actual work? How uncertain is it, how interdependent is
  it, and what skills does it genuinely demand?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. People

- Question: Who does the work? What are their skills, expectations, preferences,
  and career stage?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Formal organization

- Question: What are the structures, processes, systems, metrics, and rewards as
  designed?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

4. Informal organization

- Question: What are the culture, norms, networks, actual power, and unwritten
  rules?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 3: Score the six fits

This is the diagnostic core. Score each pair.

1. Work ↔ People

- Question: Do people have the skills the work demands, and does the work meet
  their needs and expectations?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Work ↔ Formal organization

- Question: Do our structures and processes support the work as it actually is,
  or do they obstruct it?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Work ↔ Informal organization

- Question: Does the culture help get this particular work done?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

4. People ↔ Formal organization

- Question: Do our structures and systems meet people's needs, and are they used
  as intended?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

5. People ↔ Informal organization

- Question: Does the culture fit the values of the people we employ and the
  people we are trying to recruit?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

6. Formal ↔ Informal organization

- Question: Do the stated system and the real system agree?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 4: Check the inputs

1. Environment

- Question: Have market, regulatory, technological, or funding conditions
  changed in ways our components have not caught up with?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Resources

- Question: Do we have the people, capital, technology, and reputation the
  strategy assumes?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. History

- Question: Are past decisions, precedents, and scars constraining what is
  currently possible?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

4. Strategy

- Question: Is the strategy clear enough to serve as a design criterion for the
  four components?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 5: Analysis

* **Find the worst fit, and intervene there** — not on the component that is
  easiest to change. The most common error is redesigning the Formal
  organization because it is the only component leadership directly controls.

* **Formal ↔ Informal is usually where the diagnosis lands.** A large misfit
  here means the organization has a documented system and a real system, and
  people have learned which one to obey.

* **Work ↔ Formal misfit is the classic knowledge-work problem**:
  high-uncertainty, highly interdependent work managed by structures designed
  for standardized throughput. Expect workarounds, and read them as design
  feedback.

* **A component can be excellent and still be the problem.** Excellent people in
  a badly fitting formal organization produce frustration and turnover, not
  performance. Resist the instinct to hire your way out.

* **Congruence is necessary, not sufficient.** An organization perfectly aligned
  to a market that no longer exists scores well on every fit. If Step 4
  environment scores are low, high fit scores are a warning, not a reassurance.

* **Strategy sits outside the model**, so this questionnaire will never tell you
  the strategy is wrong. If that is the live question, use
  [swot-analysis-questionnaire](../swot-analysis-questionnaire/) and
  [pestle-analysis-questionnaire](../pestle-analysis-questionnaire/) instead.
