# Westrum Organizational Culture Typology Questionnaire

To assess how information flows in your organization, use this questionnaire.
Part A is the six-item scale validated in software delivery research. Part B
places the unit in one of the three types across all seven of Westrum's
dimensions. Part C records behavioral evidence.

See
[westrum-organizational-culture-typology](../westrum-organizational-culture-typology/).

**Evidence: Good.** Unusually strong for a culture instrument. The typology was
developed from safety-critical industry case studies, then operationalized as a
six-item scale and validated in large-sample software delivery research, where
generative culture statistically predicts both delivery performance and
organizational performance. The evidence is correlational and self-report, and
the typology flattens what is really a continuum — but the chain from this
construct to outcomes is better established than almost anywhere else in this
collection.


## Before you run it

* **Team level and above.** Never individual.
* **Minimum five responses.**
* Short enough to run quarterly, which is how it is most useful — the trend
  matters more than any single administration.
* A genuinely pathological unit may report itself as generative out of fear.
  Read Part A alongside the behavioral evidence in Part C, always.

## Analysis

* **Item 2 is the load-bearing item.** "Messengers are not punished" predicts
  the rest. If it scores below 4, treat every other score as unreliable: in a
  unit where bad news is punished, a survey is also bad news.

* **"Failure leads to inquiry" is the diagnostic row in Part B.** Pathological
  cultures ask *who*, bureaucratic cultures ask *which rule*, generative
  cultures ask *what about our system*. Only the third produces learning; the
  other two produce hidden information, which is the precondition for the next
  and larger failure.

* **Mixed Part B counts are normal and informative.** Most units are
  bureaucratic with pathological edges under pressure. The dimensions that go
  pathological when the organization is stressed are the ones to work on,
  because that is when information matters most.

* **Compare Part A and Part C.** A high scale score with a Part C item 2 answer
  of "they left six months later" means the scale is measuring what people
  believe it is safe to say. Trust the behavior.

* **Part C item 5 is the fastest executive diagnostic in this repository.** Ask
  it, and then watch your own reaction. The reaction is the finding, and it is
  the mechanism by which the culture is produced.

* **One senior leader reacting badly to bad news once can undo a year of
  improvement.** That asymmetry is the whole problem, and it means the
  intervention is nearly always leader behavior rather than a programme.

* **Expect reporting to rise as the score improves**, and brief leadership
  before it does. Rising near-miss and incident reports are the signature of a
  unit becoming generative, and they will be read as deterioration by anyone not
  told in advance. See [measurement-and-metrics](../measurement-and-metrics/).

* **Pair with delivery or safety outcomes.** This is one of very few culture
  constructs with a demonstrated link to performance, so measure both and look
  at them together. See [dora-metrics](../dora-metrics/) for the software chain,
  [high-reliability-organizations](../high-reliability-organizations/) for the
  safety one, and
  [psychological-safety-questionnaire](../psychological-safety-questionnaire/)
  for the team-level counterpart.
