# Action research

Action research is the research logic underneath organizational development.
Kurt Lewin proposed it in the 1940s: knowledge about a social system is produced
by trying to change it, with the members of the system as co-researchers rather
than as subjects.

Lewin's summary: "No action without research; no research without action."

**Evidence: Moderate.** The participation mechanism is well supported: people
implement what they helped design, and participation in decision-making is
meta-analytically associated with acceptance of change. The full cycle has
rarely been tested against controls, because its logic makes controlled
comparison awkward. Strong on mechanism, weak on outcome trials.

Use when: the problem is not well understood, the people affected have knowledge
you lack, and the fix must be owned locally to survive.

Do not use when: the answer is already known and the work is implementation, or
when the situation is an emergency requiring command decisions.


## The cycle

1. **Diagnosing**: identify the problem with the people who have it.
2. **Action planning**: design a change, and state what you expect to happen.
3. **Taking action**: implement it, at a scale small enough to learn from.
4. **Evaluating**: collect data on what actually happened.
5. **Specifying learning**: name what you now believe, and feed it back into the
   next cycle.

The cycle is a spiral, not a loop: each pass should leave the system more able
to run the next pass without you.


## Why the participation matters

Participation is not politeness. It is a method claim:

* People in the system hold data no outsider can obtain.
* People implement what they helped design and resist what they did not.
* The act of collecting and discussing data is itself an intervention; the
  system changes because it is being examined.

That last point is why "just do a survey first" is never neutral. Asking a
question raises an expectation. See
[survey-feedback-method](../survey-feedback-method/).


## Variants

* **Participatory action research**: members of the system lead the research.
* **Appreciative inquiry**: an asset-based variant that studies what works
  rather than what is broken. See
  [appreciative-inquiry](../appreciative-inquiry/).
* **Improvement science / PDSA**: the health care and manufacturing lineage of
  the same idea, with tighter cycles. See
  [plan-do-study-act](../plan-do-study-act/).
* **Improvement kata**: the Toyota lineage, with an explicit target condition.
  See [improvement-kata](../improvement-kata/).


## Examples by audience

* **Health care**: a ward team measures its own interruption rate during drug
  rounds, tries a "do not disturb" tabard for two weeks, measures again, and
  decides. Clinicians accept data they collected themselves and reject data
  handed down.

* **Software**: a team forms a hypothesis that review latency, not build time,
  dominates lead time; instruments both; changes review assignment; and
  re-measures. See [dora-metrics](../dora-metrics/).

* **Executive**: a leadership team treats its own meeting as the unit of study,
  records decisions made vs. revisited over a quarter, and redesigns the forum
  based on the count.


## Limitations

* Slow relative to directive change.
* Vulnerable to the observer effect, which is also its mechanism.
* Weak external validity: what worked on one ward may not transfer.
* Requires a sponsor who tolerates learning that contradicts their prior view.
