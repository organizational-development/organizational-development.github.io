# Maturity Models Questionnaire

Maturity models are not a single model, so this questionnaire has two parts.

**Part A** is a generic capability self-assessment you can apply to any
capability you care about, scoring current level from 0 to 5.

**Part B** is a critical appraisal of a maturity model you have been handed or
are considering adopting, scoring from 1 (badly flawed) to 5 (sound). Given how
weak the evidence for maturity models is, Part B matters at least as much as
Part A.

See [maturity-models](../maturity-models/).

**Evidence: Weak.** Maturity models are descriptive conventions. Level
progression rarely predicts outcomes, and the levels are usually authored by
advocates or vendors. Use them as a shared vocabulary for a capability
conversation, never as a scorecard tied to funding.


## Part A: Capability self-assessment

Name the capability being assessed: ____________________________________

Assess against the levels below. Score the level you are **consistently** at,
not the level you have reached once.

* **Level 0 — None.** The capability does not exist and is not recognized as
  needed.
* **Level 1 — Initiate.** Ad hoc, individual, undocumented. It works because a
  specific person makes it work.
* **Level 2 — Develop.** Described and repeatable within one team. Departmental,
  not organizational.
* **Level 3 — Standardize.** Defined, documented, and used consistently across
  units. A new person can follow it.
* **Level 4 — Manage.** Measured. Performance is known quantitatively, and
  deviations are detected.
* **Level 5 — Orchestrate.** Continuously improved, and coordinated with
  partners and the wider system.

## Analysis

* **Part A: intervene where the gap between A1–A3 and A4 is largest.** A
  capability at level 3 on process and level 1 on measurement is being run on
  faith. You will not know when it degrades.

* **Level 1 capabilities that depend on one person are a risk register item**,
  not a maturity item. Name the person and the exposure.

* **Do not target level 5 by default.** A model's top level is an author's
  aspiration, not an obligation. The right target is the level at which the
  capability stops causing you problems.

* **Part B: a total below 18 out of 30 means write your own.** Build it bottom-up
  from characteristics your own people recognize, then cluster them into levels.
  A model that fits your context and was written by your own practitioners will
  be used; a model imported wholesale will be filled in and filed.

* **A low B6 score is disqualifying.** If low scores carry consequences, you will
  measure compliance and reporting behavior rather than capability. See
  [measurement-and-metrics](../measurement-and-metrics/).

* **For delivery capability specifically, prefer outcome measures to maturity
  levels.** See [dora-metrics](../dora-metrics/), which measures what a team
  achieves rather than which practices it conforms to.
