# Weisbord Six-Box Model Questionnaire

To run a Weisbord six-box diagnostic, use this questionnaire. It evaluates the
six boxes plus the environment ring, scoring each from 1 (badly broken) to 5
(working well).

The distinctive move in this model is that each box is scored **twice**: once
for the **formal system** (what the documents, policies, and org charts say) and
once for the **informal system** (what actually happens). The gap between the
two scores is the diagnosis, not the scores themselves.

See [weisbord-six-box-model](../weisbord-six-box-model/).

**Evidence: Weak.** The model has never been validated as a measurement
instrument. Treat the scores as a structured way to surface disagreement in a
group, not as measurement.


## How to run it

* Ninety minutes, mixed-level group of 8–12 people.
* Everyone scores silently and individually first, then reveals. This prevents
  the most senior or the loudest voice anchoring the group.
* Discuss the items where the group's scores diverge most widely. Divergence is
  more informative than the average.


## Step 1: Purpose

1. Formal

- Question: Is our purpose clearly documented, and does it say what business we
  are actually in?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Informal

- Question: If you asked ten people here why this organization exists, would you
  get the same answer? Does that agreement hold when resources are tight?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 2: Structure

1. Formal

- Question: Does the documented structure — reporting lines, teams, role
  definitions — fit the purpose and the work we actually do?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Informal

- Question: Does work actually flow the way the structure implies, or do people
  route around it to get things done?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 3: Relationships

1. Formal

- Question: Do we have defined mechanisms for handling conflict between people,
  between units, and between people and their technology?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Informal

- Question: When two people or two teams genuinely disagree, does it get
  resolved, or does it go underground and stay there?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 4: Rewards

1. Formal

- Question: Do our documented incentives, recognition, and progression criteria
  cover all the work that genuinely needs doing?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Informal

- Question: What actually gets someone praised, promoted, or protected here? Is
  it the same as what the formal system says?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 5: Leadership

1. Formal

- Question: Is it clear who is accountable for keeping the other five boxes in
  balance and for holding the purpose visible?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Informal

- Question: Does anyone actually do that work? When the boxes fall out of
  balance, does someone notice and act?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 6: Helpful mechanisms

1. Formal

- Question: Do we have adequate coordinating technologies — planning, budgeting,
  measurement, information systems, meetings?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Informal

- Question: Do those mechanisms help people do the work, or have they become
  overhead that people work around or quietly ignore?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 7: Environment

1. External demands

- Question: What do outside forces — funders, regulators, customers, patients,
  competitors, technology — demand of us, and do we know?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Fit with environment

- Question: Can we currently meet those demands with what we have?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Fit with individuals

- Question: Do people find it satisfying to work here, or do they comply because
  they must?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 8: Analysis

* **Compute the gap.** For each of the six boxes, subtract the informal score
  from the formal score. The box with the largest gap is where the organization
  says one thing and does another, and it is usually the highest-value place to
  intervene.

* **A large positive gap** (formal high, informal low) means the design is fine
  on paper and is not being lived. Look for why the informal behavior is
  rational: it usually is.

* **A large negative gap** (informal high, formal low) means people have
  invented something that works despite the documented system. Do not
  standardize it reflexively — first understand what makes it work.

* **Low scores everywhere on Purpose** mean stop. Nothing downstream can be
  fixed while the group disagrees about what it is for.

* **Rewards is the box most often overlooked** and most often the real
  constraint. If the informal Rewards score is low, expect every other
  intervention to be quietly defeated.

* **The model gives no causal ordering.** It tells you where friction is, not
  what to fix first. If you need cause and effect, follow up with
  [burke-litwin-causal-model-questionnaire](../burke-litwin-causal-model-questionnaire/).
