# Leavitt Diamond Model Questionnaire

To run a Leavitt diamond check, use this questionnaire. It is a fast
second-order effects check for a **specific proposed change**, scoring the
disruption to each of the four variables from 1 (no effect) to 5 (major
disruption).

Note the inverted scale. Unlike the other questionnaires here, a **high score is
a warning**, not a good result. You are looking for large unplanned effects.

Budget thirty minutes. This is a checklist, not a diagnostic.

See [leavitt-diamond-model](../leavitt-diamond-model/).

**Evidence: Weak.** A conceptual heuristic from 1965, never validated. Use it to
surface consequences nobody has thought about, then use a real diagnostic.


## Step 0: State the change

Write one sentence describing the specific change being proposed, and circle
which variable it is primarily aimed at: Task, People, Structure, or Technology.

Change: ________________________________________________

Primary target: [ ] Task  [ ] People  [ ] Structure  [ ] Technology

Leavitt's own observation: change agents choose a favorite point of entry —
technologists change technology, HR changes people, executives change structure
— and are then surprised by effects at the other three. The remaining steps
examine those three.


## Step 1: Task

1. Work content

- Question: How much does this change what the work actually is, as opposed to
  how it is done?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Judgment

- Question: How much does it change what people must decide, or transfer
  judgment from a person to a system?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Difficulty

- Question: How much does it change how hard the work is, or shift the balance
  between interesting work and exception handling?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 2: People

1. Skills

- Question: How much does it change which skills matter, and whose hard-won
  expertise becomes less valuable?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Roles and identity

- Question: How much does it change who someone is at work — their professional
  identity, status, or sense of competence?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Numbers

- Question: How much does it change how many people are needed, or which people?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 3: Structure

1. Authority

- Question: How much does it change who decides, who approves, and who can act
  without asking?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Information

- Question: How much does it change who holds information, and therefore who
  holds influence?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Boundaries

- Question: How much does it change team boundaries, handoffs, or who depends on
  whom?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 4: Technology

1. Tools

- Question: How much does it change the tools, systems, or techniques people use
  daily?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Dependency

- Question: How much does it create a new dependency on a system, a vendor, or a
  team that did not exist before?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Failure modes

- Question: How much does it change what happens when things go wrong, and
  whether people can still work?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 5: Analysis

* **Any variable scoring 4 or 5 that is not your primary target is an unplanned
  consequence.** Name an owner for it now, before the change proceeds. This is
  the entire point of the exercise.

* **A change with a high score on only its target variable is either genuinely
  contained or has not been thought through.** In practice the second is far
  more common. Ask someone who does the work daily to score it independently;
  their scores are usually higher than the sponsor's.

* **High People-identity scores predict resistance that will be misread.** Loss
  of status, competence, or professional identity produces objections that get
  described as "resistance to change" and are actually accurate reports of a
  real cost. See [bridges-transition-model](../bridges-transition-model/).

* **High Structure-authority scores mean this is a power change**, whatever it
  is being called. Power changes announced as tooling changes are detected
  quickly and resented durably.

* **This model has no environment term.** It will tell you nothing about whether
  the change is right, only about what else it will move. Pair it with a real
  diagnostic:
  [nadler-tushman-congruence-model-questionnaire](../nadler-tushman-congruence-model-questionnaire/)
  for fit, or
  [galbraith-star-model-questionnaire](../galbraith-star-model-questionnaire/)
  for structural decisions.
