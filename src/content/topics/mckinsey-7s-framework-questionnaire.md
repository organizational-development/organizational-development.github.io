# McKinsey 7S Framework Questionnaire

To run a McKinsey 7S diagnostic for your organization, use this practical
step-by-step questionnaire. It guides you through evaluating the hard elements
(strategy, structure, systems) and soft elements (shared values, style, staff,
skills), scoring alignment from 1 (poorly aligned) to 5 (fully aligned).

See [mckinsey-7s-framework](../mckinsey-7s-framework/).

**Evidence: Weak.** The model has never been validated as a measurement
instrument, there is no established scoring method, and no evidence about how
much each element matters relative to the others. Treat the scores as a
structured way to surface disagreement within a leadership team, not as
measurement.

Run it individually first, then compare. Where members of the same leadership
team score the same element three points apart, that divergence is the finding.

## Step 1: Diagnose the Hard Elements (Tangible & Structural)

   1. Strategy
   * Question: Does our strategy clearly define our competitive advantage, market positioning, and growth goals? Do our daily actions reflect this plan?
      * Rating (1–5): [ ]
   2. Structure
   * Question: Are our reporting lines, decision-making hierarchies, and team boundaries logical for executing our strategy without bottlenecks?
      * Rating (1–5): [ ]
   3. Systems
   * Question: Do our daily operational tools, IT platforms, reward tracking, and formal workflows make work easier and support our objectives?
      * Rating (1–5): [ ]
   
## Step 2: Diagnose the Soft Elements (Cultural & Human)

   1. Shared Values (Core)
   * Question: Are our core organizational values widely understood and genuinely lived by employees, rather than just written on a wall?
      * Rating (1–5): [ ]
   2. Style
   * Question: Do our leaders behave in a way that matches what they preach? Is leadership communication open, supportive, and action-oriented?
      * Rating (1–5): [ ]
   3. Staff
   * Question: Do we have the right number of people with adequate support, onboarding, and career paths to sustain our workload?
      * Rating (1–5): [ ]
   4. Skills
   * Question: Does our workforce possess the right mix of technical, digital, and interpersonal competencies to hit our strategic goals?
      * Rating (1–5): [ ]
   
## Step 3: Analysis

* **Compare hard versus soft scores.** Average the three hard S's and the four
  soft S's. A wide gap — high-scoring strategy and structure against low-scoring
  shared values and style — indicates why change initiatives face resistance.
  This is the model's single most useful output.

* **Identify friction points.** Look for specific mismatches: a new remote-first
  strategy clashing with an old-school management style, or advanced digital
  systems failing for want of workforce skills.

* **Check the spread, not just the mean.** Where the leadership team's own
  scores diverge widely on one element, the team has not agreed something it
  believes it agreed. Resolve that before designing any intervention.

* **The lowest soft score usually predicts the failure mode.** Leaders pull the
  hard levers because they are directly controllable; the soft elements then
  quietly defeat the change.

* **This model gives you no ordering.** It shows where alignment is broken, not
  which break to fix first, and it omits the external environment entirely. When
  a specific breakdown is flagged here, trace it upstream with
  [burke-litwin-causal-model-questionnaire](../burke-litwin-causal-model-questionnaire/)
  to determine whether the cause is transformational or transactional. Scan the
  environment with [pestle-analysis-questionnaire](../pestle-analysis-questionnaire/).
