# Lewin change model

Kurt Lewin's three-stage model — **unfreeze, change, refreeze** — is the
foundation of the change management field. Almost every later model is an
elaboration of it.

**Evidence: Weak.** The three-step formulation was substantially simplified
after Lewin's death, and has never been tested as a model. Later scholarship
disputes how much of it is his. The unfreeze concept is supported indirectly —
disconfirmation plus safety produces learning where disconfirmation alone
produces defensiveness — and the institutionalization requirement is supported
by the reliability with which unreinforced changes decay. Foundational, not
evidential.

Use when: you need the simplest correct mental model of change, or when you want
to diagnose which stage a stalled effort is stuck in.

Do not use when: you need operational detail. It is a frame, not a method.


## The three stages

1. **Unfreeze**: create readiness. Disconfirm the current state — show that the
   status quo is no longer viable — while providing enough psychological safety
   that people can face the disconfirmation without defending. Schein's later
   refinement: disconfirmation alone produces denial; disconfirmation plus
   safety produces learning.

2. **Change (transition)**: the movement itself. New behaviors are tried,
   competence is temporarily low, and performance usually dips. This is the
   stage most programs under-resource, because it looks like failure while it is
   working. See [satir-change-model](../satir-change-model/).

3. **Refreeze**: stabilize the new state so it becomes the default. Update
   policies, metrics, incentives, onboarding, and stories. Without refreeze the
   system reverts, usually within two quarters, and the reversion is then
   remembered as proof that change is impossible here.


## The unfreeze problem

Most change efforts begin at stage 2. A new process is announced with no
disconfirming evidence and no safety, and the organization treats it as noise.
Unfreezing is the work of establishing that the current state costs something —
with data the audience already trusts, not with slides.

The complementary error is disconfirmation without safety: relentless bad news,
public blame, or a burning-platform speech that makes people protect themselves
rather than change.


## Refreeze in a world that never freezes

The common modern criticism — organizations never stabilize, so "refreeze" is
obsolete — is partly fair and mostly misapplied. The underlying requirement
survives: a new behavior must be made cheaper than the old one, or it decays.
Read "refreeze" as *institutionalize*: the change is complete when a new person
joining next month does the new thing without being told.


## Related: force field analysis

Lewin's own operational tool for stage 1. See
[force-field-analysis](../force-field-analysis/). His key claim: reduce
restraining forces rather than increasing driving forces, because added driving
force raises tension and provokes counter-pressure.


## Examples by audience

* **Health care**: unfreezing a ward on a hand-hygiene or handover change works
  when the team sees its *own* audit data, not national averages. Refreeze
  happens when the behavior enters the ward round checklist and the induction
  pack for rotating junior staff.

* **Software**: unfreeze with the team's own delivery telemetry — lead time,
  change failure rate. Refreeze by encoding the change in the pipeline, the
  templates, and the definition of done, so the new way is the path of least
  resistance. See [dora-metrics](../dora-metrics/).

* **Executive**: executives typically unfreeze themselves months before the
  organization, then mistake their own readiness for shared readiness. The
  organization is at stage 0 when the executive is at stage 3.


## Limitations

* Treats organizations as more stable and more unitary than they are.
* Implies a linear, planned sequence; real change is iterative and contested.
* Later scholarship disputes how much of the simplified "three-step model" is
  Lewin's own formulation versus a posthumous simplification.
* Silent on individual psychology; pair with
  [bridges-transition-model](../bridges-transition-model/).
