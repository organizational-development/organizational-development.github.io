# Viable System Model Questionnaire

To run a VSM diagnostic, use this questionnaire. It assesses whether each of the
five systems exists and functions, scoring from 1 (absent or dysfunctional) to 5
(present and working).

Run it at one **recursion level** at a time — one team, or one department, or
the whole enterprise. Running it across levels simultaneously produces
confusion, which is itself a common finding.

See [viable-system-model](../viable-system-model/).

**Evidence: Weak.** Internally coherent and derived rigorously from cybernetics,
with almost no independent empirical validation. Use it to structure a
conversation about where authority and information actually sit.


## Step 0: Set the recursion level

Level being assessed: _________________________________________

- Question: What is the level above this one, and the level below?
  - Answer:

- Question: Is that answer obvious to everyone here, or contested?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 1: System 1 — Operations

1. Identification

- Question: Can we name the units that actually produce value here, as distinct
  from units that support, coordinate, or oversee?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Autonomy

- Question: Do those units have enough autonomy to handle normal variation
  themselves, without asking permission?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Viability

- Question: Is each System 1 unit itself a viable system — does it contain its
  own operations, coordination, control, intelligence, and policy?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 2: System 2 — Coordination

1. Existence

- Question: Do we have real coordination mechanisms — schedules, standards,
  shared protocols, rotas — that stop units tripping over each other?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Character

- Question: Are those mechanisms genuinely anti-oscillatory, or have they become
  commanding? Does System 2 damp conflict, or does it decide?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Escalation substitute

- Question: When two units conflict, is it resolved by a coordination mechanism,
  or by escalating to a common manager? (Score 5 for the former.)
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 3: System 3 — Control

1. Resource allocation

- Question: Is there a clear, functioning mechanism for allocating resources
  across operational units and holding them accountable?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Synergy

- Question: Does anyone actively seek synergy between units, as opposed to
  simply summing their reports?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Scope

- Question: Does System 3 stay in its lane — the inside and the now — or has it
  absorbed identity and strategy work that belongs to Systems 4 and 5?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 4: System 3* — Audit

1. Existence

- Question: Is there a channel by which leadership sees operations directly,
  bypassing the normal reporting line?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Character

- Question: Is that channel experienced as informational or as punitive? A
  punitive audit channel is corrupted and will return the answer people think is
  wanted.
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Frequency

- Question: Is it sporadic and unpredictable, as intended, or has it become a
  scheduled ritual that units prepare for?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 5: System 4 — Intelligence

1. Existence

- Question: Does anyone here have explicit responsibility for looking outside
  and forward — environment scanning, strategy, adaptation, research?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Capacity

- Question: Does that person or group have real, protected time, or is the role
  nominal and permanently crowded out by operational pressure?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Connection

- Question: Does System 4's work actually influence decisions, or does it
  produce documents that are filed?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 6: System 5 — Policy

1. Existence

- Question: Is there a body that holds identity, purpose, and values — as
  distinct from one that reviews performance?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. The 3/4 balance

- Question: Does that body actively balance today's operations (System 3)
  against tomorrow's adaptation (System 4)? Estimate the proportion of its
  agenda spent on each.
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Distinctness

- Question: Has System 5 remained distinct, or has it collapsed into System 3
  and become another operational review meeting?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 7: Requisite variety

1. Attenuation

- Question: Are we filtering incoming variety sensibly, or ignoring signals we
  should be handling?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

2. Amplification

- Question: Are we trying to handle all variety centrally, overwhelming the
  center and starving local units of the authority to absorb it themselves?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]

3. Local capacity

- Question: Do operational units have the variety-handling capacity — skills,
  authority, slack — that their environment demands?
  - Rating (1–5): [ ]


## Step 8: Analysis

* **A missing system is the diagnosis.** Score below 2 on any system's
  "existence" item and you have found the answer. Missing System 2 shows up as
  constant escalation; missing System 4 shows up as being repeatedly surprised
  by the environment; missing System 3* shows up as leadership discovering
  problems from outside the organization.

* **System 3 dominating System 4 is nearly universal.** Today's numbers crowd
  out tomorrow's adaptation, because today's numbers are urgent and measurable.
  Look at the Step 6, item 2 estimate: if System 5's agenda is more than about
  80% operational, the organization is not holding its own future.

* **System 5 collapsed into System 3** is the classic board-level failure. Every
  meeting is this quarter's performance, nobody holds identity or the now/future
  balance, and the organization will be surprised.

* **A misused platform or shared service is System 2 acting as System 3.** It
  was created to reduce friction between units and has become a control point
  that units must ask permission from. Naming which one is intended settles a
  large class of arguments. See [team-topologies](../team-topologies/).

* **Low autonomy plus low local variety capacity predicts brittleness.** The
  organization will cope until conditions become unusual, then fail suddenly,
  because all variety-handling was centralized and the center saturates.

* **Then check the level below.** VSM is recursive: a healthy enterprise made of
  units that are not themselves viable is not healthy, it is temporarily
  carried.
