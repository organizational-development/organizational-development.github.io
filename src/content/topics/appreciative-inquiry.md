# Appreciative inquiry

Appreciative inquiry (AI), developed by David Cooperrider and Suresh Srivastva,
is a strengths-based approach to change. Its premise: organizations move in the
direction of what they persistently study, so studying problems produces more
problem-focus, while studying what works when the organization is at its best
produces more of that.

**Evidence: Weak to mixed.** The published evidence is largely case studies
written by advocates, with few controlled comparisons against problem-focused
approaches. Partial indirect support comes from solution-focused practice,
from research on positive affect broadening cognition, and from the fact that
studying successes generates more actionable specifics than studying failures.
The suppression-of-the-negative criticism is substantive and should be stated
whenever the method is used.

Use when: the organization is exhausted by problem-focused change; when there is
real existing excellence to build on; when energy and hope are the missing
ingredients rather than analysis.

Do not use when: there is a serious failure requiring root cause analysis; when
harm is occurring; when the approach would suppress legitimate grievances. AI
used to avoid a real problem is dishonest, and participants will name it as such.


## The 4-D cycle

1. **Discovery** — "What gives life here?" Paired appreciative interviews in
   which people tell stories of peak experiences: a time they felt most alive,
   effective, and proud in this organization. The interview is the intervention;
   the stories carry the data.

2. **Dream** — "What might be?" Amplify the best of what is into an image of the
   future. Often expressed creatively — a headline from five years hence, a
   scene, a presentation — because propositional statements flatten the energy.

3. **Design** — "What should be?" Craft *provocative propositions* — statements
   of the ideal, written in the present tense, grounded in real examples from
   Discovery, and stretching beyond current practice.

4. **Destiny** (originally Delivery) — "What will be?" Self-organized action.
   People commit to what they will do, individually and in groups. No master
   plan, no central allocation.

A fifth, **Define**, is often prepended: choosing the affirmative topic. This
choice determines everything downstream and deserves real care.


## The affirmative topic choice

The single most consequential decision. Topics are stated as what you want more
of, not as the problem inverted.

* Not "reduce staff turnover" → "what makes people build a career here"
* Not "fix handover failures" → "handovers that leave the next team fully
  equipped"
* Not "reduce incidents" → "how we catch problems early"

The reframe is not a euphemism. It changes what people can remember and
therefore what data the interviews produce.


## The core principles

* **Constructionist**: words create worlds; the language a group uses shapes
  what it can do.
* **Simultaneity**: inquiry *is* intervention. The first question changes the
  system.
* **Poetic**: an organization is an open book; you can choose which chapter to
  study.
* **Anticipatory**: the image of the future guides current behavior.
* **Positive**: positive affect and social bonding sustain change effort.


## The AI summit

A large-group form: 100–1000 people, three to four days, running the full 4-D
cycle with the whole system in the room — including customers, patients,
suppliers, and partners. Enables enterprise-wide commitments in days rather than
months, and is expensive to run badly.


## Examples by audience

* **Health care**: an AI approach to patient experience interviews staff and
  patients about the times care felt genuinely excellent, then designs to
  reproduce those conditions. Clinicians who are exhausted by incident reviews
  often engage with this when they will not engage with another improvement
  program. Balance it with real safety work; do not substitute.

* **Software**: "tell me about the last time deploying felt effortless" produces
  more actionable design input than "what's wrong with our pipeline", because
  people describe concrete conditions rather than generalized frustration. This
  is the bright-spots move. See [switch-framework](../switch-framework/).

* **Executive**: valuable in merger integration, where problem-focused
  integration planning entrenches "us and them". Studying both organizations at
  their best creates a shared vocabulary neither side owns.


## Criticisms

* **Suppression of the negative.** The most serious charge. Participants with
  genuine grievances can experience AI as enforced positivity, and facilitators
  can use "stay appreciative" to silence dissent. Skilled practice makes room
  for difficulty inside an appreciative frame; unskilled practice does not.
* **Power blindness.** Appreciation does not neutralize hierarchy; senior
  people's stories still dominate.
* **Evidence base** is largely case-study based, and largely written by
  advocates.
* Energy generated in Dream can dissipate if Destiny lacks resourcing and
  accountability.
